A year ago, The Break didn't exist. It was an idea between a few students who wanted to do something a bit different, and £4,000 of our own money, saved up from weekend jobs, that we decided to gamble on it.

Looking back, it feels like a genuinely mad thing to have done. We had no funding, no experience running a business, and honestly, no real idea if any of it would work. We just knew we cared about the three things we started with: **community**, **environment**, and **creativity**, and figured the rest we'd work out as we went.

Almost exactly eight months in, we paid off every penny of that £4,000 start-up cost. We still can't quite believe it. That money represents a genuine risk we took on ourselves, and the fact that it's paid off, entirely because people chose to support us, means more than we can really put into words.

So, this post is mostly just a thank you.

Thank you to everyone who came and found us at BU, AUB, and the local markets we've set up at over the year. Standing behind a stall is a strange, exposing way to run a business, you get instant, honest feedback, and every single conversation, every person who stopped to chat, browse, or just say something kind, has kept us going more than you probably realise.

Thank you to everyone who's followed along on social media. Building something in public is nerve-wracking, and having people actually engage with what we're doing, rather than just watching quietly, has made this feel like something bigger than just the two of us in a flat.

And thank you to everyone who's bought something from us. Every single purchase directly funded the next step, more materials, another market stall, another litter pick. None of this happens without that support.

We've also been lucky enough to work alongside some brilliant artists this year, Amy Ryalls, Youkki, and Kai Gheorgheasa, whose creativity has shaped a huge part of what The Break has become. Getting to collaborate with people who care about their craft as much as we care about ours has been one of the most rewarding parts of this whole year.

On top of that, we got to feature in two separate magazines as an upcoming company, **Bristol247** and **SEEGUL**, which still doesn't quite feel real. Being written about by people outside our own little bubble was a strange, brilliant reminder that other people are actually paying attention.

A year in, we're still small, still figuring a lot of it out, and still running servers out of a cupboard. But we've paid off our start-up costs, built something we're proud of, and met more genuinely lovely people than we ever expected to.

Here's to year two.
