When we started The Break, we hosted with Krystal, a UK-based provider that runs on 100% renewable energy. They were - and still are - doing good work. But after we'd been running for a while, we started thinking about the bigger picture. Data centres, even renewable-powered ones, use enormous amounts of electricity. A single large data centre can consume as much power as a small town. And even though they're increasingly running on renewables, they're still part of a complex infrastructure that's fundamentally about centralisation and scale.

That got us thinking: what if we didn't need a data centre at all?
So we moved our entire website onto a Raspberry Pi - a small, cheap, reused piece of hardware sitting in a cupboard in the flat, connected to our local network. For those unfamiliar, a Raspberry Pi is a tiny computer the size of a credit card that costs around thirty to fifty pounds. It consumes between two and four watts when serving web pages, which is hundreds of times less than what a traditional data centre uses. We weren't starting from scratch with brand new equipment; we were reusing old tech that might otherwise have been binned.

The shift from a professional hosting company to running our own servers on consumer hardware wasn't about being precious or trendy. It was about following the logic we'd already committed to: if you want to be genuinely sustainable, you have to look at every layer, not just the parts that sound good on a marketing page.
But we're not stopping there.

The cupboard setup works fine for now, but it's still drawing power from the grid. Our next phase is to move the Pi outside, give it an antenna for connectivity, and power the entire thing with a solar panel. Imagine a small weatherproof box, sitting outside, running your website, completely off-grid. No grid electricity, no data centre, no carbon footprint for the infrastructure itself. It's technically complex - you need battery backup for cloudy days, a charge controller to manage the panel, and a solid connection - but it's doable. And honestly, the fact that it's a challenge is part of why it appeals to us.

We know this sounds like something from a Silicon Valley fever dream, but the reality is stranger than the hype suggests. The infrastructure we take for granted — hosting, cloud storage, streaming services - has real environmental costs that get hidden behind marketing language like "green data centres" and "carbon neutral." Those things are better than their alternatives, but they're not zero impact. An actual, genuinely off-grid, solar-powered web server? That's closer to the real thing.

We'll share more about how this actually works once we've got it running. For now, the Raspberry Pi in the cupboard is a radical downgrade from the data centre - and that's the whole point.
